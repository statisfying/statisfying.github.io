What it does

One job, done properly.

HappyStats does the statutory accounts job that accountants in growing businesses do every year, without the cost or the set-up of an enterprise platform.

Trial balance in, statements out.

Import a trial balance from any ledger. HappyStats builds the primary statements, named as your framework names them, with the statements of changes in equity and cash flows where the entity needs them, and the disclosure notes that go with them.

Mapped to the FRC taxonomy.

Every account is mapped to an element such as frs:PropertyPlantEquipment, taken from the FRC taxonomy entry point for the standard you report under, whether that is FRS 101, FRS 102 (which covers FRS 105 too) or UK-adopted IFRS.

Mappings are kept from one year to the next, so next year starts where this year finished.

Validated before you file, not after.

The iXBRL is validated against the taxonomy and the statements are checked back to the trial balance. A figure that does not reconcile is found at the mapping stage rather than by Companies House.

Nothing leaves your machine.

Entity details, trial balances and the finished accounts stay on your own machine or your shared drive. There is no server holding client data, because there is no server.

How HappyStats works

One figure, from the ledger to the filed tag.

The same four steps every year, followed here on a single number. Tangible assets of 184,210 starts as three accounts in the trial balance and ends as a tagged fact in the iXBRL that Companies House receives. Nothing in between is retyped, and every step is checked against the one before it.
